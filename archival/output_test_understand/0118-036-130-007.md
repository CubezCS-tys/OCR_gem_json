The mediating role of strategic thinking in the relationship between

mindfulness and organizational excellence among employees of the

University of Technology and Applied Sciences

Mimi Ismail a*, Ahmed Al - Badri b , Hamoud Al-Shukri c, Hashel Al-Ghafri,

Anwar Al-Shayadi, Inas Khraiba and Mohammed Omariya

Abstract:

This study aimed to reveal the mediating role of strategic thinking in the

relationship between mindfulness and organizational excellence among

employees of the University of Technology and Applied Sciences. The study

employed a descriptive methodology. The pilot study sample consisted of 60

employees from the University of Technology and Applied Sciences, while

the final study sample comprised 116 employees, distributed according to

their job type (academic and non-academic). To achieve the study

objectives, the following instruments were developed: a mindfulness scale,

an organizational excellence scale, and a strategic thinking scale. These

instruments were administered after verifying their validity and reliability. The

results showed the following: a statistically significant direct effect (p < 0.01)

of mindfulness on strategic thinking among University of Technology and

Applied Sciences employees; a statistically significant direct effect (p < 0.01)

of strategic thinking on organizational excellence; and a statistically significant

direct effect (p < 0.01) of mindfulness on organizational excellence.

Additionally, a statistically significant indirect effect (p < 0.01) of mindfulness

on organizational excellence was found. Institutional excellence is achieved

in the presence of strategic thinking as a mediating variable, and strategic

thinking also plays a partial role as a mediating variable in the relationship

between mindfulness and institutional excellence.
